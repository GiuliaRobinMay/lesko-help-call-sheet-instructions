Grant Hunt · After You Apply

You've applied. Now comes the part that wins.

Most people send their application and then go quiet. The members who actually get the money are the ones who stay organised, stay reachable, and follow up at the right moment. Here's how. 💛

Ask a coach at the Drop-In Clinic →
🧘

First — stay calm. This part is slow.

Waiting is not a sign that something has gone wrong. It's just how these offices work.

✔Most decisions take 30 to 60 days. Some take longer. Silence in week two means nothing at all.
✔A "no" is usually about their budget, not about you. Funds run out, windows close, rules shift. It isn't a judgement on you.
✔Most refusals come from missing paperwork — not from the person being undeserving. That part you can control, and this page is about exactly that.
💛

You are allowed to find this stressful. Come to a class and say so — you'll find a room full of people going through the very same thing.

📁

Keep a dossier — one folder per application

Not one big pile. One folder for each thing you applied to, named for the organization. Paper in a real folder, or digital on your computer — whichever you'll actually keep up.

📂 Toledo Housing Fund — applied 12 Aug
  • A copy of the application exactly as you sent it
  • Every document you attached — bills, proof of income, ID
  • Proof you sent it — the email, the receipt, the confirmation number
  • Every letter or email they send back
  • Your notes page: dates, names, what was said
📸

Never send your only copy. Photograph or scan every page before it leaves your hands. If they lose something — and it happens — you can send it again the same day instead of starting over.

💡

Name your files with the date. 2026-08-12 housing-fund application.pdf sorts itself in order and you'll never hunt for the right version.

🗓️

Write down every date and every name

This is the single most useful habit on this page. Every time you speak to anyone, write four things down.

1The date you called or wrote.
2The name of the person you spoke to — and their direct number or extension.
3What they told you in one line.
4When to check back — the date they gave you, or the one you chose.

A name changes everything. "I spoke with Denise on the 19th, extension 214" gets you straight to the person who knows your file — instead of explaining your whole story to a stranger again.

❓

Always end a call with one question: "When should I check back with you?" Then use that exact date. Now you're following their timetable, not guessing.

⏰

When to follow up — and how often

There is a rhythm to this. Too soon and you're a nuisance; too late and your file goes quiet. This is a sensible pattern to follow.

DAY 0 You applyWrite the date in your folder. Save the confirmation.
DAY 3–5 One short call to confirm it arrived"I'm just checking my application arrived safely." That's all. Ask how long a decision usually takes, and who is handling it.
THEN WAIT Let their window runWhatever they told you — 30 days, 6 weeks — leave them alone until it's up. Calling every few days does not speed anything up, and it's remembered.
EVERY 2 WKS After the window, a polite check-inOnce every couple of weeks. Short and friendly: your name, your reference number, "is there anything you still need from me?"
SAME DAY If they ask you for anything — drop everythingSee the next card. This is the one deadline you cannot miss.
🤝

No deadline given? Assume 30 days, then start your two-week check-ins. And always ask for a date rather than guessing one.

😊

Be the applicant they like hearing from. Short, friendly, organised, never cross. The person on the phone often has some say in what happens next — and they remember who was kind.

⚡

If they ask for more — drop everything

A request for extra information is good news — it means someone is actually looking at your file. It also comes with the shortest deadline you'll meet, sometimes as little as ten days.

🚨

A late or half-finished reply is usually an automatic no. Not a delay — a refusal. This is where most applications quietly die, and it is completely avoidable.

✔Answer within days, not weeks. Don't sit on it until the deadline.
✔Send everything they asked for in one go. Piece by piece causes confusion and delay. If they listed five things, send five things.
✔Read the letter twice before you reply, so you don't miss the small request buried in the last line.
✔Keep a copy of what you sent, and proof you sent it. A read receipt, a confirmation email, or a postal receipt.
✔Then call to confirm it arrived — and write down who you spoke to.
🙋

Don't understand what they're asking for? Ask us. Bring the letter to the Drop-In Clinic or a Q&A and a grant coach will help you read it. Far better than guessing and getting it wrong.

📬

Check your messages every single day

You can do everything else right and still lose a grant because a letter sat unopened for two weeks. Make this a daily habit while you have applications out.

✔Your email — including the spam folder. Official mail lands in spam constantly. Check it every day.
✔Your letterbox. A lot of these offices still write on paper.
✔Your voicemail. Make sure it's set up, and that it isn't full — a full mailbox means they simply can't reach you.
✔Answer numbers you don't recognise while you're waiting. It's often them.
✔Your posts here in the community, so you see it when a coach replies to you.
📞

Did your phone number or address change? Tell every organization you've applied to, straight away. Otherwise the good news goes to the wrong place.
