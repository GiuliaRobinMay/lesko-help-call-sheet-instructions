What a call sheet is

A call sheet is a list of the organizations you can contact to find grants — with the address and phone number for each one.

Every call sheet is built around two things:

✔Where you live — your ZIP code, and your state or city.
✔Your problem — the one thing you need help with.

Put those together and you get a list of real places, with real phone numbers, that help people in your situation.

⭐

Why your call sheet matters so much

Almost everything else you do here starts with your call sheet. Without one, there's nobody to call.

✔It's built for you. Not a general list from the internet — your ZIP code, your state, your problem.
✔Help is local. Most of this money is handed out county by county. That's why your ZIP code matters so much.
✔It turns "I don't know where to start" into a list. That's the hardest part, solved.
💰

A lot of this money is never advertised. It sits quietly with local offices and organizations who give it to the people who contact them and ask.

☝️

One problem = one call sheet

Always make a separate list for each problem. Never try to combine several problems into one list.

Do this — a separate sheet for each

"Help paying rent" · "Car repair help" · "Help with medical bills"

✔ Each list is full of organizations that really do that one thing.
Not this — everything in one

"Rent, my car and my medical bills"

✖ You get a thin, general list — and you won't know who to call about what.
💛

More than one problem? Wonderful. Just ask for more than one call sheet. There's no limit.

🎯

Know exactly what you're asking for

Before you join a class or ask your question, be really sure about the problem you want help with. The clearer you are, the better your call sheet will be.

If you're not sure yet, these questions will help you find it:

01What is the one problem you most need help with right now?
02If someone handed you the money tomorrow, what exactly would it pay for?
03What bill is keeping you up at night?
04What do you keep saying no to because you can't afford it?
05Is this for you, your family, or your business?
06Are you a veteran, a senior, disabled, a single parent, a caregiver, a student? Say so — each one opens doors that others can't use.
07What is your ZIP code, and your state or city?
📝

Bring three things with you: your problem in one sentence, your ZIP code, and your state. That's exactly what the team writes down when they make your call sheet.

Asking for your call sheet

Whether you ask in a class or in the Questions Channel, two things keep you safe and get you a better answer.

🔒

Never share anything personal. No phone number, no email address, no home address — nothing that could identify you. Classes and the Questions Channel can be seen by other members.

✔Be as precise as you can about the problem itself. Your ZIP code, your state and what you need are safe to share — and they're all anyone needs.
Precise, and identifies nobody

"I'm a disabled veteran in ZIP 43604 and I need help paying my rent."

🤗

Please don't be shy — show up and ask. Everyone there needed help too, and nobody is judging you. If you'd rather not be recognised, use an avatar picture and a different name, keep your camera off, and type in the chat. You get exactly the same help. 💛

🔖

Always bookmark your question. Hover over your post and click the little bookmark sign 🔖 at the top right. You'll find it again under your personal settings, in Bookmarked Posts — so you never lose your answer. You can ask as many questions as you like.

There's more about writing a good question inside the Questions Channel itself.

Keep going

One call sheet is a start. The members who do best are the ones who come back again and again.

✔A sheet for every problem. Three problems, three sheets.
✔Come back to the classes. Every coach has a different angle, and other members' questions turn up things you'd never have thought to ask.
✔Ask again whenever something changes. New problem, new sheet.
♾️

There's no limit — not on call sheets, not on classes, not on questions. Use all of it, as often as you like.
