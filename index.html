What a call sheet is

It's a list of organizations you can phone to ask for grants and financial help — with the phone number for each one.

Every call sheet is built from just two things:

✔Where you live — your ZIP code, city and state. Almost all help is local, so this really matters.
✔One problem — the specific thing you need money for.
💰

Why phoning beats searching online. Most of this money is never advertised. It sits with county offices, community action agencies, hospital foundations and utility hardship funds — places that don't run ads. They give it to the people who call and ask.

☝️

One problem = one call sheet

Never mix several problems into one list. Make a separate call sheet for each one.

Do this — four separate sheets

"Help paying rent in Toledo, OH" · "Car repair help in Toledo, OH" · "Medical bill help in Toledo, OH" · "Startup funding for a home bakery in Toledo, OH"

✔ Each list is packed with organizations that really do that one thing.
Not this — everything at once

"Rent, my car, my medical bills and my business idea in Toledo, OH"

✖ You get a shallow list — and you won't know which number to call about what.
💛

More than one problem? Wonderful. Make more than one call sheet. There's no limit, and each one only takes a few minutes to ask for.

🎯

Get clear before you ask

This is the step people skip — and it's the one that decides whether your call sheet is any good. A vague question gives you a vague list.

Before you join a class, open the AI, or post your question, know the answers to these:

1Which city and state do you need help in?
2What's your ZIP code?
3What is the one problem this sheet is for?
4If someone handed you the money tomorrow, what exactly would it pay for?
5Who is it for — you, your household, your business?
6Are you a veteran, senior, disabled, single parent, caregiver, student? Each one opens doors others can't use.
7Roughly how much money would solve it?
🤔

Still not sure? Ask yourself: What bill is keeping me up at night? That's your first sheet. What would change my life in six months? That's your second. What do I keep saying no to because I can't afford it? That's your third.

    What a good call sheet has

    Ask for 25 organizations, with these 14 details for each. If your sheet is missing them, ask for the rest!

    01Organization name
    02Website link
    03Email address
    04Phone (+ extension)
    05Contact person
    06Who qualifies
    07Areas they serve
    08Dates & deadlines
    09What they offer
    10Value of free help
    11Grant or loan?
    12Maximum amount
    13Things to watch out for
    1430-second call script
    🎲

    Ask for a mix. Government offices, non-profits, private foundations, corporate giving programs and niche organizations for your situation. A sheet that's all government agencies is missing half the money.

    Check it before you call

    AI is brilliant at finding organizations you'd never have heard of. It's less reliable at getting the phone number exactly right — testing shows about a third of AI phone numbers don't match the organization's own published number.

    That's not a reason to throw the list away. It just means ten minutes of checking first:

    ✔Open the website first. Dead link? Treat that whole row as unchecked.
    ✔Compare the phone number with the one on their own contact page. If they differ, the website is right.
    ✔Check they serve your ZIP code. If they don't, they can't help — however good they sound.
    ✔Check the dates. A closed window goes to the bottom, with a note to call back.
    ✔Flag anything odd — don't delete it. Bring it to a class instead.
    🙋

    Found a mistake? Ask a grant coach! Nine times out of ten there's a real organization behind a garbled listing, and a coach can find the right details in seconds. That's exactly what the classes are for — no question is too small.

    What to say when you call

    A call sheet sitting in your downloads folder has never paid anyone's rent. Ask these four things on every call:

    1"Do I qualify, based on where I live and my situation?"Get a yes or no before anything else.
    2"What exactly do I send you, and by when?"Write down every document and every deadline.
    3"Is there anyone else you'd recommend I call?"The best question on the whole sheet. These people know every other program in the county — one call often gives you three more names.
    4"Can I have your name and a direct number?"It makes every follow-up ten times easier.

    Write down what happened after every call. Add these columns to your sheet:

    DateWhoWhat happenedNext step
    Aug 12Denise, x214I qualify — app sentReturn form + 2 bills
    Aug 12VoicemailNo answerCall back tomorrow AM
    Aug 12Marcus, intakeWrong countyGave me 2 other numbers!
    💪

    Expect some "no"s — that's normal, and it's not about you. Funds run out and windows close. A program with no money in August may have a fresh budget in October. Ask when to call back, note it down, move to the next line.

    Then do it again

    One call sheet is a start — it isn't the method. The members who get funded are the ones who keep going.

    ✔Repeat for each problem. Four problems, four sheets.
    ✔Repeat with different words. Run the AI again for "emergency financial assistance", then "hardship funds", then "utility assistance". Different words find different organizations — and it costs you nothing.
    ✔Repeat the classes. Every coach has a different angle, and other members' questions turn up money you didn't know to look for.
    ✔Repeat your calls. Every "not right now" goes on a callback list with a date.
    ✔Repeat every few months. Programs open and close all year. A sheet from spring is out of date by autumn.
    ♾️

    There's no limit. Not on call sheets, not on classes, not on questions. Use all of it.

    🔒

    Keeping yourself safe

    Be as precise as you can about your problem — and share nothing that identifies you.

    ✖Never post your phone number, email, street address, Social Security number, date of birth, bank or card details, photos of documents, or case numbers.
    ✔Safe to share: your city, your state, your ZIP code, and what you need. That's genuinely all anyone needs.
    Precise, and identifies nobody

    "A disabled veteran in ZIP 43604 needing about $2,400 for back rent."

    ✔ Every organization will ask for the private details later, on the phone — where they belong.
    🚨

    Real program, or scam? Everything on a proper call sheet is somewhere you contact — that's the safe direction. Be suspicious of anyone who contacts you first: charging a fee to apply, saying you won a grant you never applied for, wanting gift cards or crypto, or asking out of the blue for your Social Security number. Report scams to the FTC at 1-877-382-4357.

    🤖

    A note about AI. AI can make mistakes. It can misspell a name, give a number that rings nowhere, or list a program that closed last year. Always check against the organization's own website — and if something's unclear or looks wrong, bring it to a Call Sheet Class, an AI Workshop or a Q&A and ask a grant coach. Getting your details right is exactly what they're there for. 💛
